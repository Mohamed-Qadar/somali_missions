# Speaker Notes

**Somali Global Citizen Support & Digital Consular Services Platform**  
Ministry of Foreign Affairs — Federal Republic of Somalia

_One page of talking points per slide. Timings assume a 15–20 minute briefing._

---

## Slide 1 — Cover

Good morning/afternoon, Honourable Minister, Deputy Ministers, Directors General, Ambassadors and Consuls. Today I will present a digital platform built to modernise how the Ministry of Foreign Affairs serves Somali citizens abroad — from finding their nearest mission, to booking appointments, tracking complaints, and receiving emergency assistance. Every screen you will see is a working part of the system, not a mock-up. The goal is a more transparent, responsive, and citizen-centred consular service.

## Slide 2 — The Challenge

Before showing the solution, it helps to name the problem. A Somali citizen in London, Nairobi or Istanbul who needs a passport renewed, a document authenticated, or urgent help in an emergency faces six recurring barriers: the distance to a mission, difficulty getting an appointment, no fast emergency channel, no visibility of their case, uncertainty about required documents, and — for the Ministry — limited transparency into service quality. These are exactly the gaps the platform is designed to close.

## Slide 3 — Project Vision

Our vision rests on four principles. Modern: services are digital-first and reachable from anywhere. Transparent: service statistics and case status are open, which builds public trust. Citizen-centred: the system is designed around real journeys and is fully bilingual — English and Somali. Connected: it is one national network linking every mission, the Ministry, and verified community organisations. As you can see at the bottom, the interface is fully translated into Somali and works on mobile phones, which is how most citizens abroad will access it.

## Slide 4 — Platform Overview

This is the platform’s public home. It is a single official gateway that unifies the Ministry’s consular services: a worldwide mission directory with a live map and search; appointment booking with document checklists; complaint submission and public tracking; an emergency channel; verified community organisations and events; and Ministry oversight with public transparency. The lower image shows the interactive global directory — a citizen anywhere can find their nearest mission and its services in seconds.

## Slide 5 — Citizen Dashboard

Once a citizen registers and verifies their email, they land on this personal dashboard. It summarises everything they have filed — total, active, resolved and archived — and lists each case with its live status and unique tracking code. They can file a new request in one click. Importantly, access is strictly private: a citizen sees only their own cases, enforced by the system’s role-based permissions.

## Slide 6 — Appointment System

Appointments are a core service. The citizen selects a mission and the service they need, and the system shows the real available time slots for that mission’s own calendar. Crucially, before they book, the required documents are listed — so they never travel unprepared and get turned away. The system prevents double-booking: if a slot is full, the citizen is automatically placed on a waitlist. Each confirmed appointment carries a QR code for check-in.

## Slide 7 — Complaint Management

Complaints follow a transparent workflow. A citizen submits by category, can attach evidence, and may submit anonymously. They receive a secure eight-character tracking code and can follow a live status timeline — submitted, assigned, under review, resolved — shown on the right. When the mission responds, the official public response appears on the tracking page, and the citizen can rate the resolution, which feeds the Ministry’s service-quality metrics.

## Slide 8 — Emergency Assistance

In an emergency, speed matters. The platform has a prominent emergency channel with clearly typed crisis categories — medical, arrest or detention, human trafficking, lost passport, domestic violence and more. The intake is validated so critical details are never missed, and every emergency is automatically flagged as CRITICAL priority. As we will see on the dashboards, these cases are pinned at the top of both mission and Ministry screens so they are actioned before routine work.

## Slide 9 — Community Organisations

Beyond the missions themselves, a strong diaspora support network already exists — student associations, community groups, volunteers who offer translation, accommodation and legal guidance. The platform lets these organisations register themselves and be officially verified by the Ministry. Only verified organisations appear to the public. Each manages its own profile, announcements and events through the self-service panel on the right, while the Ministry retains full oversight and moderation.

## Slide 10 — Community & Consular Events

The platform also promotes public engagement. Missions and the Ministry publish consular events — Passport Days, mobile consular services that travel to where citizens are, student orientations, document guidance and emergency-awareness sessions. Citizens can filter upcoming events by country, city and mission, see capacity, and register. This brings services physically closer to communities and strengthens the relationship between citizens and their missions.

## Slide 11 — Embassy Dashboard

This is the embassy staff workspace. The first thing an officer sees is the red band of active emergency and critical cases, which must be handled before routine work. Above the case list are live performance metrics — volume, resolution rate, average response time and citizen satisfaction — alongside SLA targets. Managers can assign cases to officers and manage status. Access is mission-scoped: staff see only the cases and data for their own mission, never another’s.

## Slide 12 — Ministry Dashboard

At the national level, the Ministry gets an operations command centre. It shows the critical crisis queue across every mission, SLA escalation alerts for cases that are running late, and a global map of distribution and performance. The second screen is the moderation console, where the Ministry verifies community organisations and approves their announcements and events before anything is shown publicly. Ministry reviewers and administrators have full national visibility, according to their role.

## Slide 13 — Transparency & Analytics

All of this activity produces evidence. For the Ministry, the analytics dashboard breaks down complaints by mission, country and category, and tracks resolution time, satisfaction and SLA compliance over time. For the public, the transparency portal — the lower image — publishes mission rankings and resolution outcomes from the same live data. Reports can be exported to CSV, Excel and PDF. Transparency is not a slogan here; it is built into the product.

## Slide 14 — Security & Data Protection

For a government platform handling citizen data, security is foundational. Access is role-based: six distinct roles each see only what they should. Staff data is mission-scoped — an officer in one country cannot see another mission’s cases, and this is enforced and tested. Sensitive actions are recorded in an audit log with user, time and IP. Anonymous complaints store no identifying data, and the public only ever sees aggregate, approved information. Authentication uses hashed passwords, email verification and brute-force lockout, and all uploads are validated. Some items — an independent penetration test and live TLS — remain for the deployment phase, and I will be transparent about that.

## Slide 15 — Benefits

The benefits reach every stakeholder. Citizens get faster service, know what to bring, and can see their own cases in their own language. Embassies get one organised workspace with emergencies prioritised and less manual follow-up. The Ministry gains real-time national oversight and evidence for decisions. For the Government, it is a modern, transparent public service that strengthens credibility. And for the diaspora, it is a trusted connection to home. Everyone the Ministry serves is better off.

## Slide 16 — Future Vision

This is a foundation, not a finished endpoint. The architecture is deliberately built to grow. The guided citizen assistant can be upgraded to AI-powered answers. The existing REST API means native mobile apps can follow. The notification system is already interface-ready for SMS and WhatsApp, so we can reach citizens on the channels they actually use. Longer term, integration with national digital identity and predictive analytics. Each of these is an addition, not a rebuild — which protects the Ministry’s investment.

## Slide 17 — Conclusion

To conclude: this platform modernises Somali consular services around the citizen. It is a real, working, secure and bilingual system that connects citizens, missions and the Ministry on one network. It is ready to demonstrate and to run in a staging environment today. A short list of items remains for full public launch — production hosting, a live security certificate, an independent penetration test and a native Somali language review — and I can walk you through that plan. Thank you for the opportunity to present it.

## Slide 18 — Thank You

Thank you. I welcome your questions, and I would be glad to give a live walkthrough of any part of the platform — booking an appointment, submitting an emergency case, or the Ministry oversight dashboards — on request.
